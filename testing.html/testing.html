🦕 Dinosaurs

A small test site about the animals that ran the planet for roughly 165 million years — and the birds that still do.

The Mesozoic in three acts

Triassic

252 – 201 mya

Dinosaurs show up small and unremarkable, sharing a hot, dry supercontinent with crocodile relatives that mostly outclassed them.

Jurassic

201 – 145 mya

Pangaea breaks apart, forests spread, and sauropods grow into the largest land animals that have ever lived.

Cretaceous

145 – 66 mya

Flowering plants, horned and armored dinosaurs, and tyrannosaurs — until an asteroid strike at Chicxulub ends the act.

Six things worth knowing

  • Birds are dinosaurs. Not descendants-of — they sit inside the theropod family tree, which makes the pigeon outside your window a living dinosaur.
  • Feathers came before flight. Many theropods had them for insulation or display long before anything used them to get airborne.
  • Velociraptor was turkey-sized. The film versions are closer in scale to Deinonychus.
  • More time separates us from T. rex than separated T. rex from Stegosaurus. Roughly 66 million years versus about 83 million.
  • The biggest sauropods likely topped 60 tonnes, with hollow, air-filled bones keeping the frame manageable.
  • The extinction was not total. Around three quarters of species died out; the survivors included the ancestors of every bird alive today.
