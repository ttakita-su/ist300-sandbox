Genre tells you what a movie is. Mood tells you whether it's right for tonight. A horror film and a documentary can scratch the same itch, while two romantic comedies can feel nothing alike. So here's a different way into our launch catalog: figure out how you feel, then pick from the list.

Every title links to its full spoiler-free review if you want more before you press play.

## Thoughtful

For when you want something to chew on after the credits.

- [Arrival]({{ '/reviews/arrival/' | relative_url }}) is a first-contact movie where the big set piece is a conversation. It's patient and quiet, and it leaves you wondering what it really takes to understand someone.
- [Get Out]({{ '/reviews/get-out/' | relative_url }}) is horror that works as social satire. Jordan Peele puts its sharpest moments in awkward small talk, not jump scares, so expect to want to talk it through afterward.
- [The Tinder Swindler]({{ '/reviews/the-tinder-swindler/' | relative_url }}) lets the women at its center describe, in their own words, how relationships that started on an app took shape. It's less a thriller than a hard look at trust online.

## Cozy

For blankets, takeout and low stakes.

- [Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone]({{ '/reviews/harry-potter-and-the-sorcerers-stone/' | relative_url }}) runs on candlelight, a school-year rhythm and a sense of belonging. It's comfort viewing built for the first cold night of the year.
- [The Proposal]({{ '/reviews/the-proposal/' | relative_url }}) starts as an office comedy, then relaxes into a big-family weekend in small-town Alaska, with Betty White stealing scenes. The beats are familiar, and that's the point.

## Romantic

For date night, or a solo night of feelings.

- [Howl's Moving Castle]({{ '/reviews/howls-moving-castle/' | relative_url }}) tucks a gentle love story inside a fairy tale, complete with painterly skies and a house that walks. Sweet without being sugary.
- [Crazy Rich Asians]({{ '/reviews/crazy-rich-asians/' | relative_url }}) is the most glamorous option here, full of lavish parties and a relationship tested by family expectations. Michelle Yeoh can end an argument with a single look.
- [10 Things I Hate About You]({{ '/reviews/10-things-i-hate-about-you/' | relative_url }}) borrows from Shakespeare for a high school romance powered by banter. Heath Ledger and Julia Stiles make sparring look like flirting.

## Tense

For when you want your pulse up.

- [A Quiet Place]({{ '/reviews/a-quiet-place/' | relative_url }}) is built on silence, so every creak on screen, and in your living room, starts to feel dangerous. Turn the volume up and keep the snacks quiet.
- [Inception]({{ '/reviews/inception/' | relative_url }}) gets tighter the longer it goes: once the heist begins, several ticking clocks run at once and the editing keeps squeezing.
- [What Jennifer Did]({{ '/reviews/what-jennifer-did/' | relative_url }}) presents a real investigation step by step, mostly through police footage and interviews with investigators. The tension is procedural, and the loss at its center belongs to a real family, so approach it with that in mind.

## Unsettling

For when you want something that follows you to bed.

- [Hereditary]({{ '/reviews/hereditary/' | relative_url }}) is a family drama about grief that keeps getting darker. Its dread comes from long, still shots and Toni Collette's raw performance more than sudden scares.
- [American Murder: The Family Next Door]({{ '/reviews/american-murder-the-family-next-door/' | relative_url }}) is told almost entirely through archival material, including texts, social media posts and police video, with no narrator steering you. That closeness to a real family is what makes it so hard to shake, so choose it with care.
