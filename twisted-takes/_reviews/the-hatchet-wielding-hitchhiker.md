## Premise

The documentary revisits 2013, when a hitchhiker known as Kai gives a wildly quotable interview to a Fresno, California, TV station about a roadside attack he says he stopped. As the clip races across the internet, the film retraces that moment through news footage and people who met him.

## The Take

The real subject is the audience. The film reminds you how fun the clip felt, then quietly asks what it means to turn a frightening real event, and the stranger caught up in it, into a catchphrase.

## What Works

Early stretches bounce along on remixes, talk-show buzz and meme montages, then the mood cools gradually instead of flipping a switch. Voices from his offline life add a texture the original clip never had.

## What May Divide Viewers

It critiques meme culture while replaying the meme constantly, and lingers on the viral fun before harder material arrives. Whether that is a deliberate trap or a padded first half depends on your patience.

## Who Should Watch

If you grew up quoting viral videos and wondering what happens to internet characters once the trend dies, start here. Media students will find plenty to argue about.
