## Premise

As the documentary presents it, the case opens in August 2018 with a call about a pregnant Colorado mother and her daughters, who cannot be reached. Every frame is something recorded before the film existed: officers' bodycam video, phone messages and the family's own online posts.

## The Take

Its boldest decision is subtraction. With no narrator and no interviews filmed after the fact, nobody explains events with hindsight. Instead, the film hands you the raw record and expects you to pay close attention.

## What Works

Shanann Watts's own videos, full of plans, jokes and worries, keep her vivid and specific on screen instead of a name in a case file. The editing stitches timestamps, texts and doorstep footage into a timeline that tightens through small details.

## What May Divide Viewers

That intimacy has a cost. Private texts and family videos, including moments with the children, end up in front of a streaming audience, and with no experts or advocates on screen, there is little wider context.

## Who Should Watch

Best for anyone curious how found material alone can carry a documentary, and for people tired of dramatized reenactments. If stories involving young children are a hard line for you, skip it.
