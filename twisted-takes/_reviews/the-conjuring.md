## Premise

In 1971, the Perron family moves into a rambling Rhode Island farmhouse, and trouble starts fast: stopped clocks, a foul smell, unexplained bruises. They call Ed and Lorraine Warren, married paranormal investigators played by Patrick Wilson and Vera Farmiga.

## The Take

There's no wink here. James Wan plays this ghost story straight, betting on suspense over blood, and that earnestness is why it works: each fright hits people you've started to care about.

## What Works

Watch the camera. Long, unbroken moves through hallways and stairwells map the house, so you always know where the dark corners are, which makes them worse. A family game of hide-and-clap becomes the film's best scare, built from little more than darkness and sound. Lili Taylor, as the Perron mother, supplies raw nerve, while wood paneling and period clothes sell the era.

## What May Divide Viewers

The ingredients are familiar: creaking doors, a music box, a creepy doll behind glass, so genre veterans may predict each scare's rhythm. The real-case framing also asks for belief some won't give.

## Who Should Watch

Groups planning a spooky-season movie night, anyone who enjoys a good haunted-house tour, and viewers who miss horror that takes its heroes seriously.
