## Premise

Twelve enormous, shell-shaped craft appear around the world, and nobody knows why. The U.S. Army recruits linguist Louise Banks (Amy Adams) and physicist Ian Donnelly (Jeremy Renner) to communicate with the visitors before nervous governments act first.

## The Take

Alien movies usually ask who will win. Arrival asks what a single word means, and somehow that's tenser. Watching Louise start from one word on a whiteboard, then explain why even a simple question needs breaking down, delivers a nerdy thrill most blockbusters skip.

## What Works

Amy Adams lets fear and curiosity flicker across her face instead of spilling into speeches. Bradford Young's camera soaks everything in fog and overcast light, so the visitors feel truly foreign. Jóhann Jóhannsson's score hums with layered voices that sound almost like breathing, and the visitors' circular, inky script is wonderfully strange.

## What May Divide Viewers

The pace is deliberate and the colors stay muted throughout. The global-politics subplot also feels sketchier than the scenes inside the shell.

## Who Should Watch

Language lovers, puzzle-solvers and anyone who prefers conversation to combat will get the most from it. Viewers who need a fight scene every twenty minutes should pick something else.
