## Premise

By day, Thomas Anderson (Keanu Reeves) works in a cubicle; by night, he's a hacker called Neo, chasing rumors about the Matrix. Then the mysterious Morpheus (Laurence Fishburne) and Trinity (Carrie-Anne Moss) offer him answers, at a price.

## The Take

This could have been a lecture about reality wearing sunglasses. Instead, every visual choice carries an idea. The chunky 1999 monitors look dated, but the logic holds: learn this world's rules and each fight becomes an argument about what a person can do.

## What Works

Color does quiet storytelling: scenes inside the Matrix carry a sickly green tint that makes even offices feel subtly wrong. Yuen Woo-ping's choreography gives the kung fu real snap, and "bullet time" freezes moments while the camera swoops around them. Hugo Weaving plays Agent Smith with a clipped, contemptuous calm.

## What May Divide Viewers

Reeves plays early Neo as a near-blank, which reads as either smart casting or flat acting. Its straight-faced seriousness can tip into unintended comedy, and the gunfights are more brutal than the styling suggests.

## Who Should Watch

Made for action fans who like philosophy with their shootouts, and anyone who enjoys a hero as confused as the audience. Best for older teens and adults; it earns its R.
