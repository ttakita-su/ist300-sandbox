How a Hand Is Played

Every player places a bet before any cards are dealt. The dealer then deals two cards to each player and two to themselves — one face up (the upcard) and one face down (the hole card).

Your Options

Hit
Take another card. You can hit as many times as you like until you stand or bust.
Stand
Take no more cards and lock in your current total.
Double Down
Double your original bet and take exactly one more card, then you are forced to stand. Most useful on totals of 9, 10, or 11.
Split
If your first two cards have the same value, separate them into two hands and place a second bet equal to the first. Each hand is then played normally.
Surrender
Offered at some tables. Give up the hand immediately and get half your bet back. Only available as your first decision.
Insurance
Offered when the dealer's upcard is an ace. It is a side bet paying 2:1 that the dealer has blackjack. It is a losing bet long-term — skip it.

Dealer Rules

The dealer has no choices. They follow a fixed procedure:

  • Hit on any total of 16 or less.
  • Stand on any hard total of 17 or more.
  • On a soft 17 (an ace counted as 11, plus 6), the dealer either hits or stands depending on the house rules. "Stands on soft 17" is slightly better for you.

Hard vs. Soft Hands

A soft hand contains an ace being counted as 11, so it cannot bust with one more card — the ace simply drops to 1. A 7 is a soft 18. A hard hand has no ace, or has an ace that must count as 1. 10 + 8 is a hard 18. The distinction matters a lot for strategy.
